Conserved Water Ledger

A what-if calculator for water conservation. It starts from the saving a program reports, the drop in water withdrawn, and follows that water to show how much the river basin actually gains, and when and where.

Illustrative numbers, not estimates

Why a reported saving can mislead

Programs usually count a conservation saving as less water withdrawn at the headgate or meter. Much of the water a use withdraws, however, flows back to the river or to an aquifer that feeds it, and people and fish downstream depend on that return flow. A practice that only cuts return flow saves the basin little or nothing. Freed water that is used again, on more acres or by a new user, can even raise the amount of water consumed.

The ledger separates these effects. It splits the reported saving into the part that would have been consumed or lost (the wet saving) and the part that would have come back anyway (the dry saving), follows the freed water to where it goes, and shows the change in river flow month by month.

How to use it

  1. Pick an example, or choose a sector and enter your own numbers.
  2. Under Hydrologic setting, say where returning water ends up.
  3. Under Water balance, enter where each unit of withdrawn water goes before and after the practice.
  4. Under Where the freed water goes, split the freed water among its possible destinations.

The results on the right update as you go. Read them from the top: the summary, the two ledgers, the monthly change in river flow, and the full accounting table. Model and assumptions, at the bottom, gives the equations.

Two ledgers

The left column is the manager's ledger: where each unit of the original diversion goes at the point of use, including any water now left in the river. The right column is the basin's ledger: the same water regrouped by what the river experiences. Water is either back in the river early, returned late through the aquifer, or depleted for good. Colors show the basin outcome. Both views always total the original diversion, so switch between Baseline and Scenario to compare.

Model and assumptions

The tool evaluates the equations of the companion paper for one use and one diversion. Equation numbers match the paper.

Water balance

Withdrawal W splits into depletion D, water lost to the basin, and recoverable return flow R. Depletion is beneficial consumption Cb, non-beneficial consumption Cn, and non-recoverable outflow N.

W=D+R,D≡Cb+Cn+N(1)

The setting decides whether each path counts as R or N. Any change between two states must balance:

ΔW=ΔCb+ΔCn+ΔN+ΔR(2)

Freed water

State 0 is before the practice, state τ has the practice in place with the freed water unused, and state 1 is after the freed water reaches its destinations. Write ΔτX=Xτ−X0. The reported saving and its wet and dry parts are

F≡−ΔτW,Fwet≡−ΔτD,Fdry≡−ΔτR,F=Fwet+Fdry.(3)

Practice type: A if the practice cuts beneficial consumption (ΔτCb<−0.05F); otherwise B if Fwet≥Fdry and C if not; D if F≈0.

Fate of the freed water

Shares λs,λu,λt,λb of F are protected, re-applied, transferred, and banked; each destination consumes a fraction di of what it receives, with ds=0. The change in basin depletion and the share that reaches the basin are

ΔD=−Fwet+F∑iλidi,ϕ≡−ΔDF(4)
  • Rebound. Re-applied water uses the new technology, so du=Dτ/Wτ, and its returns split the same way.
  • Transfer. The new user consumes dt and returns the rest on the quick path.
  • Banking. Banked water loses db to evaporation; the rest recharges the aquifer and returns on the slow-path schedule.
  • Shared diversion. Re-applied, transferred, and banked water all pass through the original diversion, so ΔW=−λsF.

Timing and location

The tool uses a two-node form of Equation (5). Diversions follow a monthly profile, quick returns arrive in the same month, and slow returns reach the river through a lag kernel h, exponential with mean L months over a steady annual cycle (* is convolution):

ΔQA(t)=−ΔW(t),ΔQB(t)=−ΔW(t)+ΔRquick(t)+(h*ΔRslow)(t)(5)

Over a year, ∑tΔQB(t)=−ΔD; Node A has no such total because return flows enter below it.

Not yet in the tool

The paper's water-availability measure, Equation (6), compares flow at each point with the flow needed there. It needs baseline river flows, which the tool does not have yet.

The examples use the paper's 100-unit district and reproduce its scenario table: fallowing gives ϕ=0.45, modernization into trust gives 0.06, and modernization with rebound gives −0.78.
